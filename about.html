About MAP · Est. 1985

Forty years of walking alongside.

MAP was founded to help Veterans and people with disabilities live more independent lives. That's still the whole job.

Mission

Michigan Ability Partners partners with Veterans and people with disabilities in support of their self-directed transformation to self-sufficiency.

Our vision is to equitably provide high-quality housing, vocational and representative payee programs and services that increase the self-sufficiency of the people we serve — and to be recognized across the region as a leading provider that works alongside other organizations to make every service better.

“MAP gives Veterans and others with disabilities hope for a better future!”

Jan Little · Chief Executive Officer
Core values

How we ride.

  1. Treat others as you'd want to be treated.

  2. Every path is personal.

    Each participant has unique goals, strengths and needs. We support their route to independence.

  3. We serve the whole community.

    Sharing resources and aligning goals with partners across the region.

  4. Our people are our strength.

    Employees and volunteers deserve training, support and fair compensation.

  5. Every dollar is accounted for.

    We steward grants, donations and fees with transparency and accountability.

  6. Lived experience shapes the work.

    We're committed to a diverse, equitable and inclusive environment for clients, staff and board.

History · 1985 → today

The route so far.

Four decades of firsts — many of them later copied across the country.

1985

The line opens

MAP launches Michigan's first vocational program to close a segregated workshop, moving every participant into a community job.

1986

Supported employment

MAP joins the first Supported Employment Initiative and helps 100 people with severe disabilities find community jobs.

1988

Downriver Ability Project

A Wayne County program for formerly institutionalized people with severe disabilities.

1989

Representative Payee begins

Born from a national NIMH project on mental health and homelessness, with financial education and credit repair built in.

1995

Housing joins the map

Vocational programs reach 1,500+ students in three counties, and MAP becomes one of three local HUD-funded permanent supportive housing providers.

1996

WISH Intensive Services

Treatment, housing, employment training and financial management under one roof for people 17 and older.

1999

Critical Intervention Project

With the University of Michigan and the VA, MAP houses 12 chronically homeless men — results later replicated nationwide. Homezone opens for Veterans.

2002

The ICAN Building System

Homeless participants help build homes with a green prefabricated system, nominated for a HUD Best Practice Award.

2005

REACH is founded

With unemployment near 10%, MAP creates work crews, two social enterprises and REACH, its affordable-housing subsidiary.

2009

Whispering Creek

REACH's first rehab becomes the community's first LEED-certified affordable housing renovation.

2014

Supportive Services for Veteran Families

VA funding lets MAP house up to 94 Veterans a year.

2015

HVRP and HireMIVet

A Department of Labor grant enrolls 192 Veterans in employment services, and the HireMIVet job-matching event begins.

2018

Key for Vets

KeyBank invests $300,000 in MAP's Veteran housing programs.

2024

Mapleview renewed

A major renovation of Mapleview follows Whispering Creek's in 2020.

2025

Forty years

MAP marks 40 years of service, with property improvements continuing into 2026.
